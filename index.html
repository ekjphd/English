Tracks

Every student earns a B.A. in English and chooses one of three tracks. Select a course to read its description.

English B.A.Creative Writing

Creative writing students choose a genre to specialize in and advance from introductory to advanced workshops, sharing original work and responding to the work of their peers.

FictionPoetryScriptwritingCreative Nonfiction

Required courses

CRW 3013Creative Writing for English Majors

An introduction to literary genres, with practice in critiquing peer writing and critical reading of established authors.

CRW 3053Theory & Practice of Creative Writing

The genres and readings of contemporary creative writing, with attention to literary technique and current issues in the field.

ENG 3014Theories and Techniques of Literature Study

Techniques of analysis, theories of interpretation, and critical approaches to literature. It is the prerequisite for all 4000-level literature courses.

WorkshopsLevel I and Level II genre workshops

Fiction (CRW 3120, 4122), Poetry (CRW 3310, 4320), Scriptwriting (CRW 3610, 4616), and Creative Nonfiction (CRW 3211, 4224). Level I pairs close reading of contemporary work with group critique. Level II offers intensive writing practice and peer critique of original manuscripts.

Lit. historyFour literature courses

At least one course in literature written before 1865 and one written after, such as American Literature I or Modern American Poetry.

Standout courses

CRW 4125Worldbuilding

Techniques for building worlds across genres. In Spring 2027, the class builds a world together and each student writes a full-length project set in it.

CRW 3713Writing for Video Games

Game design and interactive storytelling. Students outline an original game and write scripts for its cut scenes and scripted events.

CRW 4724The Florida Review

Hands-on experience in editing, publishing, and producing UCF's national literary journal, including evaluating real submissions from working writers.

English B.A.Literature

Literature students learn to read discerningly, think critically, and write clearly. They study literature in its historical, cultural, and theoretical contexts, from the Middle Ages to the present.

Required courses

ENG 3014Theories and Techniques of Literature Study

Techniques of analysis, theories of interpretation, and critical approaches to literature. It is the prerequisite for all 4000-level literature courses.

LIT 3212Research & Writing About Literature

Research, writing, and critical analysis skills for upper-level literature courses. Students may take a creative writing or technical writing course instead.

Lit. historyFive literature courses

At least two courses in literature written before 1865 and two written after, such as English Renaissance Poetry and Prose, Narratives of Slavery, or Post-World War II Fiction.

AuthorA major author course

An in-depth study of one major author, such as Shakespeare, Chaucer, or Milton, or a course in Major American, British, or World Authors.

LanguageA linguistics or theory course

For example, Principles of Linguistics, History of the English Language, Modern English Grammar, or Practical Criticism.

Standout courses

ENL 4333Shakespeare Studies

Reading, analysis, and discussion of Shakespeare's plays, with attention to his language, cultural context, and themes such as kingship, love, and revenge.

ENL 3296Gothic Literature

Gothic prose, poetry, and drama from the British Isles and the cultural contexts that produced them.

LIT 3933Literature and Law

Law, crime, and punishment in literature. Recent sections have examined vigilantes, avengers, and how literature defines justice.

LIT 3192Caribbean Literature

How Caribbean societies have achieved self-expression through documentary writing, fiction, and popular culture.

English B.A.Technical Communication

Technical communication students learn to write, design, and edit documents, websites, and visuals that make complex information clear, and they complete projects for real clients.

Required courses

ENC 3241Writing for the Technical Professional

Writing effective correspondence, instructions, proposals, and reports in the role of a professional technical communicator.

ENC 4280Technical Writing Style

Strategies for understanding and improving style across a variety of technical writing genres.

ENC 4293Documentation and Project Management

Development of a book-length project from idea to published product, such as a style manual or training manual.

ENC 4294Client-Based Project Management

Team-based planning, design, and development of documents for an industry or nonprofit client, ending in a final published product.

LIT 4433Literature of Science and Technology

Major works of fiction and nonfiction about the history, philosophy, politics, and new ideas of science and technology.

ENC 4215 / 4218Document Design or Visual Technical Communication

Either the design and publication of technical documents, or the design of informational graphics and visual displays of technical information.

Standout courses

ENC 4290Usability Testing for Technical Communication

Usability theory and techniques for evaluating manuals, online documentation, and other technical communication.

ENC 4265Writing for the Computer Industry

Creating software manuals, online help systems, and other documentation for programming, software development, and information technology.

ENC 4414Writing and Hypertext

Writing and HTML/CSS coding for online environments, hypertext architecture, and electronic literacy.
